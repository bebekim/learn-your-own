# Digest — "Would I have gotten that reward? Long-term credit assignment by counterfactual contribution analysis"
Meulemans, Schug, Kobayashi, Daw, Wayne (ETH Zürich / DeepMind / Princeton), NeurIPS 2023.
Source: papers/longtermcredit.txt. Anchors: paper §/eq/fig + [txt:L<line>] for grep. Reader target: LYO counterfactual credit design.

## 1. Problem formulation

**What it is.** COCOA (Counterfactual Contribution Analysis) is a family of model-based policy-gradient algorithms that credits each *action* $a$ taken in *state* $s$ for its influence on each later *rewarding outcome* $u'$ (an encoding of a state-action-reward triplet, Def. 1 [txt:L208]), over an undiscounted infinite-horizon MDP with a zero-reward absorbing state (§2 [txt:L103-127]). The driving query is interventional, not associational: *"would the agent still have achieved the rewarding outcome if it had taken another action?"* (§1 [txt:L83-95]). Horizon: all future timesteps $k\ge1$ simultaneously — time itself is abstracted away via a geometric prior $p(K=k)=(1-\beta)\beta^{k-1}$, $\beta\to1^{-}$ (App C.1 [txt:L1064-1094]); with discounting, $\beta=\gamma$ (App J, Eq. 120-121 [txt:L2681-2696]).

**Core stance.** Replace the time-proximity heuristic with measured contribution. Rearranged discounted REINFORCE shows discounting *is* a credit-assignment heuristic — each reward reinforces past actions $\propto \gamma^{t-k}$ (Eq. 1 [txt:L143-144]):
$$\hat\nabla^{\text{REINFORCE},\gamma}_\theta V^\pi(s_0)=\sum_{t\ge0}R_t\sum_{k\le t}\gamma^{t-k}\nabla_\theta\log\pi(A_k|S_k)$$
COCOA substitutes learned contribution coefficients $w$ for the $\gamma^{t-k}$ weights (§3.2, Eq. 30 [txt:L1259-1261]).

**(a) vs. TD credit assignment.** TD/Q-learning estimates the *full expected sum* of future rewards; variance of that sum over long horizons → bias in value estimates needing exponentially many updates (§1 [txt:L39-42]); TD(λ)/eligibility traces credit by temporal proximity (App A [txt:L1026-1033]). COCOA never estimates a value function: it measures contribution of an action to *individual* rewarding outcomes, so it can assign $w=0$ to outcome classes the action does not influence — disentangling subtasks and ignoring uncontrollable/distractor reward variance (§1 [txt:L92-95], §4.1 [txt:L451-456]). Closest TD relative: expected eligibility traces (van Hasselt et al. [81]), which also credit counterfactual predecessor states.

**(b) vs. HCA.** HCA [1] is the direct parent: hindsight ratio $p_\pi(a|S_t=s,S'=S_{t+k})/\pi(a|S_t)$ measures how important $a$ was for reaching a future *state* (Eq. 2 [txt:L151-158]). COCOA generalizes the conditioning target from states $s'$ to arbitrary rewarding-outcome encodings $u'=f(s',a',r')$; $u'=s'$ recovers HCA (their simplified, reward-model-free variant is HCA+) (§3.1 [txt:L227-231], §3.2 [txt:L252-253]). The paper's central negative result: state-conditional contributions are **spurious** — fine-grained states decode the action sequence, so HCA over-credits and degrades to REINFORCE (Prop. 2, §3.3 [txt:L274-278]); reward- or reward-object-feature encodings fix this (Thm. 3). HCA-return (return-conditioned baseline) is shown *biased* even with ground-truth hindsight unless an unrealistically strong support condition holds (App L [txt:L2898-2933]; fails already on a 2-arm bandit).

**(c) vs. Woulda-Coulda-Shoulda / counterfactual policy search (Buesing et al. [43]; Mesnard et al. [3]).** That line does Pearl-*counterfactual* (rung-3) reasoning: infer the posterior over exogenous noise variables $\{N\}$ given the observed trajectory $\tau$, then intervene *holding the noise fixed* — "given everything else remains the same" (App I.2 [txt:L2605-2619]). WCS does this with a learned differentiable SCM + pathwise derivatives → **continuous actions only** (App A [txt:L1001-1014]). Mesnard et al. amortize the noise posterior into a summary statistic $\Phi(\tau)$ fed to a hindsight baseline. COCOA's "counterfactual" is rung-2 *interventional*: its coefficient corresponds to a do-intervention on the MDP's causal graph (App I.1, Eq. 119 [txt:L2591-2604]):
$$w_{Do}(s,a,u')=\frac{p_\pi(U'=u'\mid S_t=s,Do(A_t=a))}{\sum_{\tilde a}\pi(\tilde a|s)\,p_\pi(U'=u'\mid S_t=s,Do(A_t=\tilde a))}-1$$
and because conditioning on $s$ satisfies the backdoor criterion for $U'$ w.r.t. $A$, do() = observational conditional — so COCOA is estimable from plain on-policy data, works with **discrete actions**, but averages over environment randomness instead of conditioning on the realized noise. App I.2 sketches upgrading to true trajectory-counterfactuals via $\Phi(\tau)$-style summaries [txt:L2620-2665].

## 2. The contribution measure

**Definition (Eq. 3, §3.1 [txt:L213-226]).** The counterfactual contribution coefficient of action $a$ in state $s$ upon future rewarding outcome $u'$:
$$w(s,a,u')=\frac{\sum_{k\ge1} p_\pi(U_{t+k}=u'\mid S_t=s,A_t=a)}{\sum_{k\ge1} p_\pi(U_{t+k}=u'\mid S_t=s)}-1=\frac{p_\pi(A_t=a\mid S_t=s,U'=u')}{\pi(a\mid s)}-1$$

**The contrast.** Numerator: probability of encountering $u'$ at *any* future time (possibly multiple times) after taking $a$ in $s$, then following $\pi$. Denominator: same quantity under the **policy mixture** — $p_\pi(U'=u'|S=s)=\sum_{a'}\pi(a'|s)\,p_\pi(U'=u'|s,a')$ — i.e., the counterfactual actions are drawn from the current policy itself. Ratio-minus-one form ⇒ sign interpretation: $w>0$ ($<0$) means $a$ raises (lowers) the probability of $u'$ relative to behaving as usual; $w=0$ ⇒ no contribution. RHS (Bayes) is the **hindsight form**: how over-represented was $a$, relative to its propensity $\pi(a|s)$, among trajectories that later hit $u'$.

**What it is NOT.** Not leave-one-out over event sets; not Shapley (no coalition averaging, no interaction terms). It is a *pairwise* (single action vs. policy mixture at one state) relative-lift measure; multi-event credit is handled by summing pairwise coefficients over (past action) × (future outcome) pairs, which is exactly why the combinatorics never explode (§3.2). Thm. 4 gives an "occurrence" reading: $w(s,a,u)=O^\pi(u,s,a)/O^\pi(u,s)-1$ where $O^\pi(u,s)=\sum_{k\ge1}p_\pi(U_k=u\mid S_0=s)$ is the expected number of future encounters (App C.6, Eq. 55 [txt:L1444-1447]).

**Choice of $u'$ matters more than the formula.** $u'=r'$ (scalar reward) → lowest variance; $u'=f(s',a')$ learned reward-object features → disentangles aliased rewards; $u'=s'$ (HCA) → spurious contributions (§3.1 "Choice of rewarding outcome" [txt:L227-231]).

## 3. The estimator

**Policy-gradient estimator (Eq. 4, §3.2 [txt:L235-241]):**
$$\hat\nabla^U_\theta V^\pi(s_0)=\sum_{t\ge0}\nabla_\theta\log\pi(A_t|S_t)\,R_t+\sum_{t\ge0}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\nabla_\theta\pi(a|S_t)\sum_{k\ge1} w(S_t,a,U_{t+k})\,R_{t+k}$$
Two structurally important properties: (i) it evaluates **all counterfactual actions** (the $\sum_a \nabla\pi(a|S_t)$ term), not only observed ones; (ii) contribution × reward is **multiplicative** — outcomes with $w\approx0$ are gated out, instead of being averaged in as with additive control variates (§4.1 [txt:L451-456]). Variants: HCA-style with reward model (App C.3, Eq. 26); sampled-action version for large/continuous action spaces, $M$ actions $a^m\sim\pi(\cdot|S_t)$ drawn *independently of the trajectory actions* (Eq. 27 [txt:L1230-1240]).

**Learning $w$ from observations (§3.4 [txt:L353-391]).** Three routes:
1. **Hindsight classification (used in all main experiments).** Train $h(a\mid s,u',l)$ to classify the observed action $A_t$ given current state $s$ and a *future* outcome $u'=U_{t+k}$, cross-entropy over all $(t,k)$ pairs along trajectories (Alg. 1, App E.1 [txt:L1611-1615]); then $\hat w = h/\pi - 1$. Policy logits $l$ are an extra input to track the moving, policy-dependent target (Prop. 5 [txt:L381-382]: $p_\pi(a|s,u',l)=p_\pi(a|s,u')$). Architecture: hypernetwork with multiplicative interaction with the policy logits — useful inductive bias since for zero-contribution actions the hindsight distribution equals the policy (App E.8.1 [txt:L1896-1916]).
2. **Contrastive density-ratio (App D.2, Prop. 7 [txt:L1488-1501]).** Binary classifier $D(a,s,u')$ separating $a\sim p_\pi(\cdot|s,u')$ vs. $a\sim\pi(\cdot|s)$:
$$\mathcal L=\mathbb E_{s,u'\sim\mathcal T(s_0,\pi)}\big[\mathbb E_{a\sim p_\pi(\cdot|s,u')}[\log D]+\mathbb E_{a\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}[\log(1-D)]\big],\qquad w=\frac{D^*(a,s,u')}{1-D^*(a,s,u')}-1=e^{x}\;\text{(logit }x\text{)}$$
3. **Successor representations (App D.3 [txt:L1532-1562]).** $\sum_k p_\pi(S_{t+k}=s'|s,a)=M(s,a,s')$ is Dayan's SR, learnable by TD; recombine into outcome coefficients: $\sum_k p_\pi(U_k=u'|s,a)=\sum_{s'}p(u'|s')M(s,a,s')$ with policy-independent $p(u'|s')$. Used for ground truth via DP recursion $M(s,a,s',t)=\sum_{s''}p(s''|s,a)\sum_{a''}\pi(a''|s'')\big[\mathbb 1_{s''=s'}+M(s'',a'',s',t-1)\big]$ (App E.2, Eq. 75-78 [txt:L1575-1634]).

**Reward-object features $u=f(s,a)$ (App E.8.3 [txt:L1925-1960]).** Train a reward predictor (MSE + L1 sparsity to discard state detail irrelevant to reward), take the penultimate layer, discretize/bin it. Requirements: fully predictive of reward (Thm. 1) yet minimal extra information (Thm. 3) — a deliberate bottleneck.

**Handling combinatorics.** (i) Time: geometric-$K$ marginalization collapses "at which future step" into one time-independent coefficient (App C.1); training pairs are just all $(s_t, u_{t+k})$, no alignment search. (ii) Actions: exact $\sum_a$ for small spaces, else independent action samples. (iii) Outcomes: credit is per (action, outcome-class) — outcome vocab stays small by design of $u'$.

## 4. Assumptions and validity conditions

**Fully-predictive encoding (Def. 2 [txt:L257-261]).** $U$ must be a sufficient statistic for reward:
$$p_\pi(R_k=r\mid S_0{=}s,A_0{=}a,U_k{=}u)=p_\pi(R_k=r\mid U_k{=}u),\ \text{independent of }k$$
**Thm. 1 [txt:L262-267]:** if $U$ is fully predictive and $w$ are the ground-truth coefficients of Eq. 3, the COCOA estimator (Eq. 4) is unbiased: $\nabla_\theta V^\pi(s_0)=\mathbb E_{\mathcal T(s_0,\pi)}\hat\nabla^U_\theta V^\pi(s_0)$. Proof route (App C.2 [txt:L1109-1212]): Lemma 6 — the coefficients decompose the advantage,
$$A^\pi(s,a)=r(s,a)-\sum_{a'}\pi(a'|s)r(s,a')+\mathbb E_{\mathcal T(s,\pi)}\Big[\sum_{k\ge1} w(s,a,U_k)R_k\Big]$$
then the policy-gradient theorem. **Required conditions:** Markov state $s$ (backdoor: action⊥future outcome confounding given $s$, App I.1); support $\pi(a|s)>0$ wherever the hindsight form is used (ratio form stays defined even at $\pi=0$, App C.1 [txt:L1104-1108]); proper MDP (absorbing state reached w.p.1), bounded rewards (App B); **on-policy** data — the hindsight distribution is policy-dependent, so off-policy $w$ is biased unless corrected (§3.4, Discussion [txt:L540-545]); imperfect $\hat w$ ⇒ biased gradients, but see robustness below.

**What the other results guarantee.**
- **Prop. 2 [txt:L274-278]:** if each action sequence leads deterministically to a distinct state, HCA+ = REINFORCE exactly ($p_\pi(a|s,s')=\delta(a{=}a_t)$, App C.4 Eq. 31-33). Pixel/continuous/POMDP state spaces have this property to a large extent (App G [txt:L2152-2161]).
- **Thm. 3 [txt:L299-307]:** in a simplified setting (policy optimized at one initial step, reward only at final step), with $S$ fully predictive of $U'$, $U'$ of $U$, $U$ of $R$:
$$\mathbb V[\hat\nabla_R]\preceq\mathbb V[\hat\nabla_U]\preceq\mathbb V[\hat\nabla_{U'}]\preceq\mathbb V[\hat\nabla_S]\preceq\mathbb V[\hat\nabla_{\text{REINF}}]\quad(\text{PSD order})$$
Proof = law of total variance over nested conditional expectations (App C.5). Interpretation: **coarser outcome encodings (less info about prior actions) ⇒ strictly lower variance**; verified beyond the simplified setting in the tree environment (Fig. 2, Fig. 7 [txt:L1373-1380]).
- **Thm. 4 [txt:L331-352]:** each credit term drives up the expected *number of encounters* of the encountered $u'$; over-informative $u'$ "forgets" unvisited sibling outcomes ⇒ variance. Mechanistic explanation of Thm. 3.
- **App K (bootstrapping) [txt:L2707-2850]:** n-step returns possible (Thm. 10) but the bootstrap term needs *state-based* coefficients $w_n(s,a,s')$ — reintroducing spurious contributions; open problem. Also contains a correction to HCA's Thm. 7 (collider bias in their graphical model).
- **App L [txt:L2898-2933]:** HCA-return unbiased only if every return reachable from $s_t$ is also reachable from $(s_t,a_t)$ — a support condition violated even in bandits. Warning for any estimator that conditions hindsight on the *return/final outcome* and uses it as a baseline.
- **Robustness (App F.4, Fig. 12 [txt:L2140-2151]):** COCOA-reward's SNR degrades gracefully under persistent multiplicative perturbations $(1+\sigma\epsilon)$, $\sigma$ up to ~0.1; sensitivity comparable to Q-critic, better than Advantage/HCA-state.

**Degeneration of COCOA itself (App G.2-G.3 [txt:L2216-2269]).** Deterministic *continuous* rewards make each state uniquely decodable from its reward ⇒ COCOA → HCA-like variance. Remedies: bin outcomes, or stochastic encodings $U\sim\mathcal N(R,\sigma)$ — explicit bias-variance dial; equivalently, add noise to $u'$ when training the hindsight model.

## 5. Empirical findings

Setup: small MDPs where **dynamic programming yields ground-truth policy gradients**, so estimators are compared on bias/variance/SNR $=\|\nabla V\|^2/\mathbb E\|\hat\nabla V-\nabla V\|^2$ (§4 [txt:L407-410]), disentangling estimator quality from model-learning error.

- **Linear key-to-door** (1-D track: pick up key at $t{=}0$, stochastic distractor apples, open door → treasure; distance 100; Fig. 3 [txt:L367-377]). With ground-truth coefficients, COCOA-reward matches a *perfect* Q-critic and solves the task almost immediately; HCA/HCA-return barely beat REINFORCE. With learned models: COCOA-reward/-feature most sample-efficient; learned Q-critic lags (full action-value is harder to learn than coefficients). HCA's failure is diagnosed: the Markov state contains the has-key bit, so HCA credits key-pickup for *distractor apple rewards* — spurious contributions bury the treasure signal (§4.1 [txt:L445-449]). COCOA's variance even beats TrajCV (SOTA hindsight control variate), attributed to multiplicative $w\times r$ gating vs. additive baselines that must model the full return.
- **Scaling the delay (Fig. 1B [txt:L61-67]).** Increasing key↔door distance: all baselines' performance and SNR decay (~1/distance, cf. Hung et al. [4]); COCOA's SNR stays roughly flat.
- **Task interleaving (Fig. 4 [txt:L465-505]; App E.7 [txt:L1842-1890]).** Simplified Mesnard et al.: interleaved contextual bandits, reward for a choice arrives later together with its context tag (C=5 contexts, B=3 open, O=2 objects, dying prob $1-\gamma=0.05$). COCOA beats all baselines; coefficient heatmaps show it learns "actions in context $c$ contribute only to rewards in context $c$" (w≈0 cross-context); HCA does not disentangle.
- **Reward aliasing (Fig. 5 [txt:L496-521], §4.3).** Give the treasure the same scalar value as one distractor value: COCOA-reward can no longer distinguish the outcomes and degrades; COCOA-feature (learned object features) is robust. ⇒ The identity of $u'$, not its scalar value, must separate outcome classes.
- **Reward switching (App F.2, Fig. 10 [txt:L2061-2115]).** Treasure turns poisonous at 60k episodes: COCOA-feature adapts almost instantly (coefficients invariant, only $r(u)$ re-learned); COCOA-reward adapts quickly (only one outcome's coefficients to relearn); Advantage/Q-critic must re-update all upstream values. Disentangled credit = localized re-learning.
- **Where it struggles:** (i) deterministic continuous rewards (App G.2, above); (ii) data scarcity — hindsight model trains only on on-policy *rewarding* observations, acute under sparse rewards (Discussion [txt:L540-551]; App H on latent learning / information-bottleneck representations $Z$ that enable recombination $w(s,a,u')=\sum_{s'}p_\pi(s'|s,u')w(s,a,s')$, Prop. 9 [txt:L2481-2492]); (iii) COCOA-reward under aliasing; (iv) bootstrapped n-step variants (App K). RUDDER/Align-RUDDER are **not** benchmarked — discussed only as heuristic reward-transport (App A [txt:L992-1000]); the paper positions COCOA as the principled replacement for such heuristics.

## 6. Transfer analysis — LYO's symbolic setting

LYO deltas vs. paper: discrete interventions (lessons), binary per-cycle outcomes, no learned dynamics, no differentiable policy — but: logged Thompson propensities, per-injection receipts, replayable event log. Key realization: **the hindsight form of Eq. 3 needs no dynamics model at all** — $w = \hat p(\text{inject } \ell \mid \text{context } s,\ \text{outcome } u')/\rho_\ell - 1$ is a pure density ratio estimable from receipts alone (a classifier or stratified rates), with the logged propensity $\rho_\ell$ as the denominator. That is the transferable core.

**(a) A better within-run credit rule than "all pending injections get the outcome."**
- Adopt the per-injection coefficient $w_i=\hat h(\ell_i\mid s_i,u')/\rho_i-1$, where $\hat h$ predicts *which lesson (or null) was injected* at decision point $i$ from (context, terminal outcome class). Update Beta posteriors **gated/weighted by $w_i$**: irrelevant lessons get $w\approx0$ → no update (this is exactly the multiplicative gating that beat TrajCV's additive averaging, §4.1) — fixing over-crediting directly, unlike equal-share or leave-one-out splits.
- Credit *pairwise* (injection $i$ × outcome class $u'$), not coalitionally — Thm. 3's warning says fine-grained joint attribution inflates variance; COCOA deliberately avoids Shapley-style interaction accounting. If interaction between two co-injected lessons matters, treat "no lesson" as the null arm in the same classifier rather than computing subsets.
- Also credit **counterfactual arms**: COCOA's $\sum_a\nabla\pi(a|s)\,w\,r$ term updates all actions, including lessons *considered but not injected* — LYO's logged propensities make the mixture available; at minimum, failed runs should count as negative evidence against the injected lesson AND mildly negative for its propensity neighborhood.
- Do **not** copy HCA-return (condition on final outcome, use as baseline): App L shows that estimator class is biased under realistic support conditions. Use the ratio/coefficient form, which evaluates counterfactuals and stays unbiased.
- Time handling: don't decay credit by cycle distance (γ-as-heuristic critique, Eq. 1); sum contributions over all later cycles (geometric-$K$ marginalization, App C.1). If within-run delay truly matters, App J's discounted form $w_\gamma$ (Eq. 120) is the principled version.

**(b) Sharpening the lift estimator ("would this run have passed without the lesson").**
- COCOA's contrast distribution is the **propensity mixture at the same context**: $p(u'|s)=\sum_{a'}\pi(a'|s)p(u'|s,a')$. LYO's lift should therefore be $\frac{P(\text{pass}\mid s,\ell)}{\sum_{\ell'}\rho_{\ell'}(s)\,P(\text{pass}\mid s,\ell')}-1$ — a *propensity-standardized relative lift* within matched context strata (same task/failure class), not a raw global difference in rates. Logged propensities + Thompson randomization give the backdoor condition (inject ⊥ potential outcomes | s, ρ) for free — the same argument that makes do() = conditional in App I.1.
- Prefer the **ratio-minus-one** form over difference-in-rates: sign semantics ($w\gtrless0$), multiplicative gating of null contributions, and direct use as fractional Beta counts.
- Choose $u'$ coarse-but-fully-predictive (Thm. 3): binary pass/fail is the lowest-variance target — but Fig. 5's aliasing result says binary is too coarse when different failure modes share it. Recommended $u'$ = (validator failure class, pass) — the "rewarding object features" analogue; never anything as fine as log hashes or exact final state (HCA-degradation trap, Prop. 2 / App G).
- Scope note: this is the *interventional* (population) counterfactual — the right target for Beta-posterior lift. The unit-level "would *this* run have passed" is App I.2 territory (noise-conditioned counterfactual), requiring logged seeds/latents + replay; COCOA does not deliver it either.

**(c) Data to start logging NOW.**
1. **Full propensity vector per decision** (all candidate lessons *plus the null/no-inject arm*, not just the chosen one) — needed for the mixture denominator; Beta parameters per arm suffice to reconstruct it.
2. **Null-injection decisions and negative examples** — cycles where no lesson was injected, and failed runs with tags. Both estimator routes (hindsight classifier, contrastive ratio, Prop. 7) need samples from $\pi(\cdot|s)$ and from non-outcomes; today these are the likeliest gaps.
3. **Decision-point context $s_i$**: task id, validator failure class, cycle index, retry count — the backdoor conditioning set; without it, lift is confounded by difficulty (Thompson assigns lessons more often where failures persist).
4. **Per-cycle outcomes, not only run-final**: the training pairs are $(s_t, u_{t+k})$ for *all* $k\ge t$ (Alg. 1, App C.1); cycle-level validator tags enable time-abstracted within-run credit instead of one terminal label.
5. **Outcome-class identity per cycle** (failure taxonomy), to support the anti-aliasing $u'$.
6. **Bandit posterior snapshot / propensity-provenance id** (Prop. 5 analogue): the hindsight target drifts as Thompson posteriors update; conditioning on (or at least versioning) the bandit state keeps the estimation target stationary and makes off-policy receipts correctable.
7. **Run randomness**: seeds, temperature, model/checkpoint ids, tool-call trace hashes — the exogenous-noise record $\{N\}$ (App I.2) that a future Φ(τ)-style, trajectory-level counterfactual ("replay this exact run without the lesson") would require. Cheap to log, impossible to reconstruct later.

**Caveats for LYO.** Unbiasedness (Thm. 1) needs $u'$ fully predictive of the outcome (satisfied if pass/fail is determined by its class tag) and propensities $>0$ (Thompson guarantees this; clip tiny $\rho$ since $w\sim1/\rho$). Estimation error in $\hat h$ ⇒ biased credit, but Fig. 12 shows graceful degradation under multiplicative error — reassuring since LYO's updates are Beta counts, not gradients. On-policy restriction: retrain/refit $\hat h$ as the bandit drifts, or importance-correct old receipts.
